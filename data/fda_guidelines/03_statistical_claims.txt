FDA PROMOTIONAL GUIDELINES — STATISTICAL AND NUMERICAL CLAIMS
Source: FDA Guidance on the Presentation of Quantitative Efficacy and Risk Information
Version: 2024

RULE 3.1 — REQUIRED STATISTICAL CONTEXT
All numerical efficacy claims must include:
a) The specific statistical measure (p-value, confidence interval, or hazard ratio)
b) The patient population in which the result was observed
c) The trial phase and design (e.g., "in a randomized Phase III trial")
d) The primary endpoint being measured

RULE 3.2 — P-VALUE PRESENTATION
When citing p-values in promotional materials:
- The exact p-value must be stated (e.g., p=0.03, not just "p<0.05")
- P-values must be from the pre-specified primary analysis
- Post-hoc or exploratory p-values must be labeled as such
- A p-value of 0.05 does not constitute statistical significance per FDA standards

RULE 3.3 — HAZARD RATIO REQUIREMENTS
Claims based on hazard ratios must include:
- The hazard ratio value (e.g., HR=0.71)
- The 95% confidence interval (e.g., 95% CI: 0.61-0.83)
- The reference comparator (e.g., "versus placebo")
A hazard ratio without confidence intervals is considered incomplete and misleading.

RULE 3.4 — RELATIVE VS ABSOLUTE RISK
When presenting risk reduction data:
- Relative risk reduction (RRR) claims must be accompanied by absolute risk reduction (ARR)
- Presenting only relative risk reduction without absolute risk reduction is misleading
- Number needed to treat (NNT) should be provided when clinically meaningful

RULE 3.5 — ROUNDING AND APPROXIMATION
Rounding of efficacy data in promotional claims is only permitted when:
- The rounding does not change the clinical interpretation
- The rounded figure is labeled as approximate
- The precise figure is available in accompanying materials

RULE 3.6 — COMPARATIVE STATISTICS
Claims comparing drug performance to placebo must clearly state "versus placebo"
or "compared to placebo." Claims comparing to active comparators must cite
the specific comparator drug and its approved dose.
