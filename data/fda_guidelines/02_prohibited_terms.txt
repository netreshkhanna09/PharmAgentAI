FDA PROMOTIONAL GUIDELINES — PROHIBITED TERMS AND ABSOLUTE CLAIMS
Source: FDA Guidance for Industry — Presenting Risk Information in Prescription Drug Advertising
Version: 2024

RULE 2.1 — ABSOLUTE CLAIMS PROHIBITION
Promotional materials must not use absolute claims of safety or efficacy.
The following terms are prohibited when used to imply absolute benefit:
- "cure" or "cures"
- "eliminates" (when referring to disease)
- "100% effective" or similar absolute efficacy claims
- "completely safe" or "totally safe"
- "miracle drug" or "miracle treatment"
- "breakthrough" (unless accompanied by regulatory designation)
- "revolutionary" (unless supported by comparative evidence)

RULE 2.2 — SUPERLATIVE CLAIMS
Superlative claims ("the most effective", "the best", "the safest") require
direct comparison data from head-to-head clinical trials. Without such data,
superlative claims are considered misleading and are prohibited.

RULE 2.3 — IMPLICATION OF SUPERIORITY
Claims that imply superiority over competing drugs without direct comparative
clinical trial evidence are prohibited. This includes claims like "a new option
for patients who have failed other treatments" when no head-to-head data exists.

RULE 2.4 — VAGUE EFFICACY LANGUAGE
Vague terms that imply efficacy without substantiation are prohibited:
- "works better"
- "more powerful"
- "stronger"
- "faster acting" (without time-to-onset data)
These terms require quantification and clinical substantiation.

RULE 2.5 — SURVIVAL CLAIMS
Claims involving survival benefit (Overall Survival, Disease-Free Survival,
Progression-Free Survival) require robust Phase III data with survival as
a pre-specified primary or key secondary endpoint. Survival claims based on
post-hoc analyses or surrogate endpoints require explicit qualification.
