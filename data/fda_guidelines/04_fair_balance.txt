FDA PROMOTIONAL GUIDELINES — FAIR BALANCE AND RISK INFORMATION
Source: 21 CFR 202.1(e)(5) and FDA Guidance on Presenting Risk Information
Version: 2024

RULE 4.1 — FAIR BALANCE REQUIREMENT
All promotional materials that make efficacy claims must present risk information
with the same prominence as the efficacy claims. This is called "fair balance."
A promotional piece that prominently features efficacy data but minimizes
or omits risk information violates the fair balance requirement.

RULE 4.2 — REQUIRED SAFETY INFORMATION
Every promotional claim must be accompanied by or reference:
a) The most serious risks (boxed warning if applicable)
b) The most common adverse reactions
c) Contraindications
d) A statement directing healthcare professionals to the full prescribing information

RULE 4.3 — ADVERSE EVENT CONTEXT
When citing efficacy statistics, the promotional material must not omit
context about adverse events that occurred at similar rates. For example,
claiming "Drug X improved Overall Survival" without noting that Grade 3-4
adverse events occurred in 45% of patients is considered misleading.

RULE 4.4 — PATIENT POPULATION CONTEXT
All efficacy claims must clearly identify the patient population in which
the results were observed. Applying results from a specific subpopulation
to the broader indication is misleading.

RULE 4.5 — HEADLINE CLAIMS
Headline or headline-equivalent claims (those appearing in large text or
prominently placed) are subject to heightened scrutiny. The benefit must
be clear, the risk context must be immediately adjacent, and no misleading
implication may arise from reading the headline alone.

RULE 4.6 — TONE AND EMPHASIS
Promotional materials must not use layout, formatting, or typography to
minimize risk information relative to efficacy claims. Risk information
must be presented in the same font size and with similar visual weight
as the efficacy information it accompanies.
