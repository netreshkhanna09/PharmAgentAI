FDA PROMOTIONAL GUIDELINES — SUBSTANTIATION REQUIREMENTS
Source: 21 CFR 202.1 and FDA Draft Guidance on Substantiation of Promotional Claims
Version: 2024

RULE 1.1 — SUBSTANTIATION REQUIREMENT
All promotional claims for prescription drugs must be supported by substantial evidence
or substantial clinical experience. A claim is considered unsubstantiated if it is not
backed by at least two adequate and well-controlled clinical investigations.

RULE 1.2 — STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE THRESHOLD
Claims of efficacy based on clinical trial data must demonstrate statistical significance
with a p-value less than 0.05. Claims citing p-values of 0.05 or greater must be
qualified as "not statistically significant" or must not be presented as demonstrating
efficacy. A p-value of exactly 0.05 does not meet the threshold for statistical
significance under FDA standards.

RULE 1.3 — CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Statistical significance alone does not establish clinical significance. Promotional
materials must not imply clinical meaningfulness from statistical significance alone
without supporting evidence of clinically meaningful benefit.

RULE 1.4 — PRIMARY ENDPOINT ONLY
Efficacy claims must be based on pre-specified primary endpoints. Claims based solely
on secondary or exploratory endpoints require explicit qualification and are generally
not acceptable as standalone efficacy claims in promotional materials.

RULE 1.5 — EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
Phase III randomized controlled trials represent the gold standard for substantiation.
Claims based on Phase I or Phase II studies must be clearly labeled as preliminary
or exploratory and must not imply established efficacy.

RULE 1.6 — SINGLE TRIAL EVIDENCE
A single clinical trial may be sufficient for substantiation only when the trial is
particularly persuasive — typically a large, well-designed, multicenter Phase III
study with a highly statistically significant result (p less than 0.001).
